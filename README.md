# 100 Unequal People · 100 ongelijke Belgen · 100 Belges inégaux

A single-page site that makes inequality in Belgium visible by putting the
whole country in one room: **100 fictional adults, one per percentile**,
built from real income and wealth statistics. Visitors first guess where
they stand, then enter their own numbers, and see the gap between the two.

Live: https://olivierpierre85.github.io/100-unequal-people/

The goal is the *perception gap*: almost everyone, cashiers and surgeons
alike, believes they are "somewhere in the middle". The site exists to show
Belgians both ends of the ladder they are standing on.

## What the page does

1. **Where would you land?** Two sliders for the visitor's guesses ("I think
   I am better off than N of the 100" on income, and on wealth), then net
   monthly income, optionally how much of it comes from wealth, and their
   share of net wealth. Each money field has a fold-out calculator (13th
   month, holiday pay, meal vouchers, mortgage, shared with a partner…)
   that fills the field in. The answer shows their actual rank on both
   measures, the distance from each guess, and a ladder per measure.
   Nothing is sent anywhere; the numbers stay in the browser.
2. **The parade.** All 100 people as bars on a deliberately linear scale,
   toggling between net wealth and net monthly income. In the income view
   each bar is split into "from work & pensions" and "from wealth" (rent,
   dividends, interest). Hover or arrow keys to meet each person, click to
   pin them. The visitor's own marker is drawn in the parade.
3. **The 100 as a row of people** under the parade, in the same order and
   coloured by wealth, scrolling sideways. The visitor's own figure is
   inserted at their value in coral, and the neighbours are drawn bigger
   the closer they are, so the people just above and below you are easy to
   pick. Every person has an emoji avatar derived from their job.
4. **A person's card.** Job, region, language, income and wealth, where the
   income comes from, and five everyday comparisons derived from the
   numbers: home, holidays, eating out, a new phone, a big concert.
5. **Four numbers**, the "Nobody thinks they're rich" explainer, the full
   table (the accessible twin of the chart, folded away by default) and the
   method and sources.

Everything is in **Dutch, French and English**: the language is picked from
`?lang=`, then a saved choice, then the browser language, and can be
switched in the header.

## Data

`src/data/profiles.js` is generated by `node scripts/generate-profiles.mjs`
(deterministic, seeded). Anchor values are illustrative interpolations of
Statbel salary and fiscal statistics and the NBB/ECB Household Finance and
Consumption Survey, scaled to one adult; the script documents each source.
Highlights of the model:

- One person per income percentile (0.5 … 99.5). The richest of the 100
  stands for the *average* of the top 1%, about €14,500 net a month and
  €4.8M net wealth; Belgium's richest families are ~1000× wealthier still.
- Wealth rank is correlated with income and age. The five highest earners
  are also the five wealthiest, so the income-from-wealth split in
  `src/data/incomeSources.js` (≈3.2% net yearly return on wealth beyond the
  family home, capped at 85% of income) never assumes a fortune yields more
  than its owner earns.
- No pensioner lives on less than ~€1,300 a month, the level the income
  guarantee for the elderly (IGO/GRAPA) tops a single person up to. The
  lowest percentiles are students, part-timers and jobseekers on cohabitant
  benefit rates.
- All 100 first names are unique. Job titles are emitted as keys and
  translated (and gendered, for French) in `src/data/jobs.js`.
- `src/data/lifestyle.js` maps income and housing to the everyday
  comparisons; the sentences live with the other copy in
  `src/i18n/messages/*.js`.

`doc/` holds an earlier CSV draft that is not used (see `doc/README.md`).

## Tech

Vue 3, Vue Router, Tailwind, Vite. Fonts (Bricolage Grotesque, Nunito) are
self-hosted through Fontsource and bundled; no requests to Google Fonts, no
analytics.

```
npm install
npm run dev        # local dev server
npm run build      # production build in dist/ (also writes dist/404.html)
node scripts/generate-profiles.mjs   # regenerate the 100 (SEED=… for another draw)
node scripts/generate-og-image.mjs   # regenerate public/og.png (share image)
node scripts/smoke-test.mjs          # server-render the page in NL/FR/EN, fail on errors
```

### Deploying to GitHub Pages

Pushing to `main` runs the *Deploy to GitHub Pages* workflow, which builds
the site and publishes `dist/` to the `gh-pages` branch. The base path is
set to `/100-unequal-people/` automatically inside GitHub Actions.
`dist/404.html` is a copy of `index.html`, so deep links and refreshes on
GitHub Pages reach the client-side router instead of a dead page.

Old URLs keep working: `/profiles?view=income` and `/wealth-pyramid`
redirect to the home page with the matching view.
